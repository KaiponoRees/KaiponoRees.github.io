Throughout college, two of my biggest interests have been software engineering and the Air Force. At first, I viewed these as two separate parts of my life, with software engineering being what I study academically and the Air Force being the career I hope to pursue. However, as I have gained more experience in both ICS and AFROTC, I have started to realize how much the two can connect.

Software engineering has taught me how to approach problems logically and find solutions when something does not work the way I expect it to. At the same time, AFROTC has challenged me to become a better leader, communicator, and teammate. Both require me to solve problems, work with other people, and continue learning from my mistakes. Moving forward, I hope to continue developing both sides of myself and eventually use the skills I gain from each throughout my career.

## My Interest in Software Engineering

One of the things that interests me most about software engineering is problem-solving. I like being given a problem and having to figure out how I can use code to create a solution. Sometimes that process can be frustrating, especially when I cannot immediately figure out why something is not working, but finally getting my program to work makes the struggle worth it.

My ICS classes have also shown me that software engineering is about much more than just knowing how to code. I have been learning how important communication, teamwork, organization, and being able to understand other people's code are. As I continue through the program, I want to become more confident working on larger projects where I have to combine these skills instead of only working on smaller individual assignments.

## My Future in the Air Force

The Air Force is another major part of what I hope to do in the future. Through AFROTC, I have already had opportunities to experience leadership and learn what it means to be responsible for more than just myself. Being placed in leadership positions has challenged me to communicate clearly, manage my time, hold myself and others accountable, and make decisions when other people are depending on me.

These are skills that I want to continue developing because I know becoming a good officer will take more than simply knowing how to complete a task. I want to become someone who can understand a problem, make a decision, communicate a plan, and help the people around me succeed. I also want to gain more experience working with different types of people because everyone communicates, learns, and responds to leadership differently.

## Combining the Two

What interests me about my future is seeing how software engineering and the Air Force can connect. Technology is constantly becoming more important, and the Air Force relies heavily on software, communications, cybersecurity, data, and other computer-based systems. Because of this, I think having a background in ICS can give me skills that will be valuable during my Air Force career.

At the same time, I think my Air Force experience can make me a better software engineer. Working in teams, communicating under pressure, managing my time, and taking responsibility for my work are skills that apply to both fields. Even if I am not personally writing code every day in the Air Force, understanding technology and knowing how to approach technical problems could help me make better decisions and work more effectively with the people who specialize in those areas.

## Skills I Want to Build

There are still many things I want to improve before beginning my career. On the software engineering side, I want to become a stronger programmer and gain more experience working on larger projects. I also want to improve at debugging and become more comfortable finding solutions without immediately needing help when I run into a problem.

For the Air Force, I want to continue developing my leadership, communication, decision-making, and time-management skills. One thing I have learned through AFROTC is that becoming a better leader takes experience. I can learn about leadership in a classroom, but actually being put into leadership positions allows me to figure out what works, what does not, and what I need to improve.

## Looking Toward the Future

I do not know exactly where software engineering or the Air Force will take me yet, but that is also what makes the future exciting. Right now, my goal is to take advantage of the experiences I have in both ICS and AFROTC and continue improving myself in both areas.

In conclusion, I hope to leave college with more than just a degree or the ability to write code. I want to have the technical skills to understand and solve difficult problems, while also having the leadership and communication skills to work effectively with others. Software engineering and the Air Force may challenge me in different ways, but I believe the experiences I gain from both will help prepare me to become a better software engineer, leader, and future Air Force officer.
